Project

Rilo

An iPhone screen-time buddy. A panda that gets you off your phone and gives you something better to do instead.

  • Native iPhone app built
  • Apple Developer approved · Nov 2026
  • Next: Screen Time permission, then TestFlight
  • 42+ on the waitlist
Join the waitlist (opens in a new tab)

The problem

Screen-time apps just block you, so people turn them off.

What I built

A panda buddy that learns your hobbies and goals. When you open a limited app, he asks what it’s for and points you at something better to do.

A buddy that asks why

Open a limited app and Rilo asks what it’s for, then nudges you toward something you actually like doing instead.

Ideas from your hobbies

Rilo learns your hobbies and goals and suggests real things to do, like shooting hoops or working on your project, instead of scrolling.

Your day, planned

Tell Rilo when you’re free, when bed is and what has to get done. He plans the rest of your day, with just enough scroll time to stay under your goal.

Rewards for staying off

Earn XP for time off your phone, level up, and unlock 23 panda skins.

Limits that stick

Set how many times you can open each app and for how long. Strict mode makes loosening a limit wait 24 hours and stretches the pause before an app opens from 5 to 15 seconds.

Talk to Rilo

Chat by voice or text. It runs on Apple’s on-device AI, so it stays on your phone.

What I learned

Real apps take patience.

Building the web demo took days. Getting it onto an iPhone took weeks. I paid for my Apple Developer account on November 7 and it sat on ‘Pending’ for over two weeks with no reply. I kept building anyway, so when it got approved, I was ready.

Make it your own.

My first version of Rilo looked a lot like a friend’s app. It worked, but it didn’t feel like mine. So I redesigned it around a panda with its own personality, moods and look. Now nobody confuses the two.

Feedback beats your own opinion.

I thought my onboarding was great. My first testers said it was way too long and the home screen was too busy. I cut it way down, and Rilo got better the moment I stopped defending my ideas and started listening.
