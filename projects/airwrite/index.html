Project

AirWrite

Write on any table with your finger. No paper, no pen.

  • Runner-up · Hack the Track
  • Built solo
  • Sustainability theme
  • Sept 11–12, 2026
See the code (opens in a new tab)

The problem

Built for a sustainability hackathon. You can take notes, sketch and write without using a single sheet of paper.

What I built

AirWrite turns a bare table into a notebook. Two cameras track your fingertip. One watches from above, the other sits at table level and sees when your finger touches the surface. Everything you trace shows up as digital ink in AirWrite’s notebook, where you can save your docs and export them.

Write on any table

No paper or tablet. Just trace on a bare table with your finger.

Two-camera tracking

One camera above follows your fingertip, one at table level checks how high your finger is, so ink only shows when you touch the table.

Digital ink, live

Your movements appear in the notebook in real time as smooth digital ink.

Save and export

Save your docs in the notebook and export them as PDFs.

Handwriting to text

Claude reads your writing back as typed, searchable text you can fix before exporting.

Touch you can tune

The side camera measures how high your fingertip is above the table. Sliders set where the table is and how close you have to get before ink starts, or rest your finger down to set it automatically.

What I learned

  • A bare finger tracks better than a held pen. The hand-tracking model is looking for a finger.
  • Where the camera sits matters more than the code. From above, hovering and touching are a pixel or two apart, so the side camera has to sit at table level.
  • Raw hand tracking jitters. A One Euro filter keeps the line still while you hover and quick while you write.
